Claim B (source: D003, E008): Meridian forensic report shows Ostrow's credentials accessed the Kestrel core/research repository subdirectories (containing the unfiled torque-compensation source code and tuning notebooks) on 11 occasions between March 18-April 30, 2025, and that he transferred Kestrel_Core_Algorithm_v4.2.zip and Torque_Compensation_Source.tar.gz (nearly 1.2GB combined) to a personal USB device on April 30, 2025.

Materiality: Directly bears on witness credibility and the knowledge/access element of the trade-secret misappropriation claim.

C3: Identical unusual technical language/misspelling in Vantek's unfiled confidential draft vs. Halcyon's independently published documentation. High

Claim A (source: D005): Vantek's internal, unreleased Kestrel Rev. C draft (March 2025) contains an engineering note with the misspelling "threshhold."

Claim B (source: D005): Halcyon's publicly released Falcon-9X documentation (June 2025 beta) contains the identical misspelling "threshhold" plus matching coefficients (k1=0.087, k2=0.014) and near-identical algorithm description, despite Halcyon disclosing no independent tuning methodology or dataset.

Materiality: Strong circumstantial evidence undermining any independent-development defense; central to the misappropriation/copying theory and damages case.

MEDIUM Severity

C2: Characterization of access as restricted/exceptional vs. standing/routine. Medium

Claim A (source: D006, E016): Exit interview suggests Ostrow's access to core algorithms was not his to have ("that was Priya's call to keep tight"), implying no standing access.

Claim B (source: D003): Forensic report finds Ostrow's role (Senior Firmware Engineer) had standing read/write access to the core/research subdirectories and that this access "was not exceptional or the result of a permissions error."
